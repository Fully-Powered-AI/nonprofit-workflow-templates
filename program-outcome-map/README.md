# Program outcome map

Before an evaluation, the program and evaluation leads get one map linking each activity to the outcome it is meant to serve, with proposed measures, data sources, assumptions and the questions the evidence cannot yet answer. It claims no impact.

*Workflow template · Nonprofits · Analytics and reporting · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/program-outcome-map/)*

## The outcome

Everyone preparing for the evaluation looks at the same page. Each activity sits beside the outcome it is meant to serve, with the measures you already collect, the measures worth adding, the data each would come from and the assumption that has to hold for the activity to lead to the outcome.

The map is honest about what a count can show. Sessions delivered and people reached say the program ran; they do not say anyone changed. The page keeps those counts apart from measures of a condition, labels what each link actually has behind it, and turns every gap into a numbered question: what the figures cannot tell you about a starting point, about other things happening at the same time, about who was not heard and about effects nobody planned for.

The leads decide. They approve or change each proposed measure, confirm or drop each link the assistant had to propose, and choose which further evidence to collect. The assistant draws the map and claims no impact, decides no one's eligibility for help and works only from de-identified or supplied program information.

## Who starts the work, and when

The program lead or the evaluation lead starts it when an evaluation, a funder review or a program redesign is coming and the team wants to agree what is being tested before anyone collects more data. Run it again when an outcome changes, a new measure is added or fresh data arrives.

## What they supply

- **The activities:** what the program does, how much of it, how many people it reaches and over what period, with any outcome the plan already names for it.
- **The intended outcomes:** each in the plan's own words, for whom, by when, and where the plan states it.
- **The measures you already have:** what each counts, where the data comes from, how often it is collected and its latest figure.
- **Any starting figures,** where a baseline was taken.
- **Participant feedback** that participants have approved for use, without names or contact details.
- **Your settings:** the program name, the evaluation and its date, the leads, the data period and anything else happening at the same time that might move the outcomes.

## What comes out

One page in a fixed order: the totals; the map, a block for each link from an activity to an outcome; activities with no outcome and outcomes with no activity; the counts that are not outcome evidence; the proposed measures; the numbered questions the evidence cannot answer; the decisions for the leads; and a check that every activity, outcome, measure and feedback item is accounted for.

## What has to change for your company

Decide who the two leads are and who answers each kind of question. Use your own names for activities, outcomes and measures, and keep their identifiers stable from one run to the next. List the other influences you want asked about, such as a policy change or a partner's new service, each with the outcomes it might touch. Agree what counts as approved feedback and who confirms the approval. Keep the file names and column headings your team already uses, or map them once.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
