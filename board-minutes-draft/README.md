# Board minutes draft

After a board meeting, the secretary gets draft minutes built only from what they supply: notes, agenda, motion text and vote record. Attendance, motions, votes, documents and actions are laid out, and every gap is marked for the secretary to settle.

*Workflow template · Nonprofits · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/board-minutes-draft/)*

## The outcome

The secretary opens a complete first draft instead of a blank page. It follows the agenda, lists who was in the room and when they came and went, sets each motion down word for word as supplied, shows the vote as the record gives it, names the documents the board looked at, and lists every action with its owner and date.

Where the record is thin, the draft says so in the place it matters. A vote with no result written down, a decision that appears in the notes but in no motion, an action nobody owns, a closed session with no notes: each is marked and listed at the end, so the secretary spends the time on those few points rather than on typing. The draft states no result the record does not give, and it gives no view on whether a meeting had a quorum or a vote was valid.

The secretary verifies every line against what they know of the meeting. The board approves the minutes. Until then it is a draft.

## Who starts the work, and when

The board secretary starts it soon after the meeting, while memory of the room is fresh, once the notes, the vote record and the list of who attended are in hand. The secretary corrects the draft, resolves each marked gap with the chair or the people concerned, and circulates it in the way the board already does.

## What they supply

- The secretary's notes, in whatever order they were taken.
- The agenda: item number, title, presenter and documents.
- The roster and attendance: each person, whether they vote, their status, and arrival and departure times.
- The motion and vote record: motion text exactly as it was put, who moved and seconded, the votes for, against and abstaining, any recusal, and the result as the chair announced it.
- The list of documents the board saw, with a reference, title and date for each.
- The meeting details: organization, date, place, chair, secretary, times and the next meeting.

## What comes out

One draft in a fixed order: the meeting details, attendance, the proceedings item by item, the documents referred to, the actions, a blank approval line, the gaps for the secretary, and a check that the counts agree with the inputs. Each motion appears with its wording, movers, tally and recorded result. Each gap is marked where it occurs and listed at the end.

## What has to change for your company

Decide how full your minutes are: decisions and actions only, or a short account of discussion. Decide whose names appear against remarks; the default is movers, seconders, presenters and action owners only. Set the wording your board uses for the heading, the call to order, the quorum statement and the approval line, and keep that wording in your own hands. A school or college board that works under open-meeting or public-records rules should keep its required wording and format in its own hands too. Agree who confirms a result the chair did not write down, and how quickly the draft goes to the chair. Provide last approved minutes as a sample of your house style.
