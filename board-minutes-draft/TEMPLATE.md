# Board minutes draft

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Draft the minutes of one board meeting from the secretary's notes, the agenda, the roster and attendance, the motion text and vote record the secretary supplies, and the list of documents the board saw. The draft records attendance, each motion word for word with its movers and tally, the documents referred to and the actions, in agenda order, and marks every gap for the secretary. The assistant prepares; it words no resolution beyond the text supplied, states no result the record does not give, gives no opinion on quorum, on whether a vote was valid or on what governance rules require, and never sends, files, signs or approves anything. The secretary verifies the record and the board approves the minutes.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it after a board meeting, once the secretary has the notes, the vote record and the attendance in hand, to produce the first draft of the minutes.

Do not use it:

- to prepare the board's reading before a meeting, or the CEO's record of the decision the board was asked to make; use board meeting pack;
- to prepare an agenda and a one-page record for an ordinary working meeting; use meeting agenda, notes and follow-ups;
- to keep a running register of decisions across meetings; use decision log;
- to advise on quorum, on whether a vote was valid, on bylaws or on legal duties, which belong to the board's own counsel or advisers;
- to write a resolution, to improve or tidy motion wording, or to fill in a vote or result that was not recorded;
- when there are no notes and no vote record, because the draft would have nothing to stand on.

## Roles

- **Runs it and verifies every line:** the board secretary.
- **Confirms results, wording and attendance the record leaves open:** the chair.
- **Owns each action once the board has approved the minutes:** the person named against it.
- **Approves the minutes:** the board, at a later meeting.

## Inputs

1. **Meeting details.** Organization, board name, meeting date, place or format, chair, secretary and title, time called to order, time adjourned, next meeting, run date, minutes style (default: action minutes, meaning decisions, motions and actions with at most three sentences on each item), and name rule (default: names appear against movers, seconders, presenters and action owners only; other remarks are recorded without a name). Attendance facts (who was present, arrived, left, was recused or was out of the room) are not remarks and carry the person's name. An individual abstention or vote is not printed by name: the Votes line gives the counts and any recused names. Any missing detail is written "[not supplied]" and listed as a gap.
2. **Agenda.** One row per item. Default headers: Item, Title, Presenter, Documents. If a header does not match, stop and ask which supplied column stands for it; never guess.
3. **Roster and attendance.** One row per person on the roster, voting or not. Default headers: Name, Role, Voting, Status, Arrival, Departure, Notes. Status is Present, Absent or Excused. Blank Arrival or Departure for a present person means present throughout. If the Voting column is missing, stop and ask.
4. **Motion and vote record.** One row per motion. Default headers: Motion, Item, Time, Moved by, Seconded by, Motion text, For, Against, Abstain, Recused, Result, Notes. Recused holds names; blank means none. Result is what the chair announced and the secretary recorded; it may be blank. Time may be blank. If there are no motions, say so; an empty record is allowed.
5. **Documents.** One row per document the board saw. Default headers: Ref, Title, Version or date, Item.
6. **The secretary's notes.** Free text, in any order, in the secretary's own shorthand.
7. **A sample of the board's last approved minutes** (optional), used only for headings and order of presentation, never for content.

The assistant works from files and notes the person supplies. It needs no connection to any board portal, calendar or mail system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm the headers. Count agenda items, roster rows, motions and documents. If a motion number or an agenda item number appears twice, stop and ask. If a motion names an item that is not on the agenda, set the row aside as a gap and carry on.
2. **Match names.** Notes often use first names. Match a first name to the roster only when exactly one person on it has that name. Otherwise write the name as the notes give it and add a gap. Use full roster names everywhere else.
3. **Write the meeting block** from the details. Do not fill a missing detail from the notes or from memory of other meetings.
4. **Write attendance.** Group the roster by Voting and Status. Show arrival and departure times where they differ from the called-to-order and adjourned times, and any note. Count voting members present against voting members on the roster. Then write "Quorum statement: [for the secretary to complete]". Never write that a quorum was or was not present.
5. **Place the notes.** Match each part of the notes to an agenda item by number or title. Summarise in the past tense, in plain words, at most three sentences per item, using only what the notes say. Copy figures as the notes give them. Add no adjective of judgment, and do not write "unanimously", "after discussion", "resolved" or "it was agreed" unless the motion text or the record says so. Apply the name rule. A part of the notes that matches an item by its content counts as fitting that item, even when it gives no number or title; place it under that item. Anything in the notes that fits no item at all goes under "Other matters in the notes", with a gap.
6. **Write each motion under its item.** Give the time, the mover and the seconder from the record, the motion text in quotation marks exactly as supplied (not corrected, shortened or reworded), the votes for, against and abstaining, and any recused names. Give the result exactly as the Result column has it. If the Result is blank, print "[RESULT NOT RECORDED: G#]". Never work out a result from the tally, and never say a motion passed or failed.
7. **Check each vote.**
   - Count the voting members who were present at the motion's time: Status Present, Arrival at or before the time (blank means before the meeting began), Departure after the time (blank means after the meeting ended). If the time is blank, write "presence at the vote not checked" and add a gap.
   - For + Against + Abstain + the number of recused names should equal that count. If it does not, give both numbers in a gap and change neither.
   - If the notes name a different mover or seconder from the record, print the record's and add a gap giving both.
   - If a mover, seconder or recused person is not on the roster, was not present at the time, or is not a voting member, add a gap saying so. Draw no conclusion from it. The exception is a recused person whom the notes say left the room for that vote: add no gap for that alone.
8. **Decisions with no motion.** If the notes describe a decision, an agreement or a direction that has no motion row, record it in the item as "Noted from the secretary's notes, not recorded as a motion", in the notes' own terms, and add a gap. Do not write motion text for it.
9. **Closed session.** Use the closed-session line of the output format: the times in and out, and the people the notes say were or were not present. Write a time the notes do not give as "[not stated: G#]"; never put another time, such as the time people returned or the adjournment time, in place of a missing one. Add nothing about content unless the secretary supplied a line labelled for the minutes. If a time or the list of those present is missing, or there are no notes, add a gap.
10. **Documents.** List every document in the list. Show its reference against the item that cites it. If a document is cited in the notes and not on the list, or is on the list with no item, add a gap. If the notes give a figure, period or date that does not agree with the title or date of the listed document for that item, add a gap giving both and settle neither.
11. **Actions.** Take actions only from the notes. Give owner, action and due date as the notes give them. If the owner is missing, write "Owner not stated"; if the due date is missing, write "not stated"; one gap covers everything missing from one action. A due date given in words, including a condition that is not a date (such as "before the December meeting" or "when the landlord's copy arrives"), is printed as given, with no gap. Number actions A1, A2 in the order they appear in the draft: by agenda item, and within an item in the order of the notes. Assign nothing and invent nothing.
12. **Close the draft.** Leave the approval lines blank for the secretary. List every gap, numbered G1 onwards in the order they occur in the draft, each saying where it is, what is missing or does not agree, the words or figures as supplied and who can settle it. Run the check: counts of items, motions, documents, actions and gaps; the voting members present at each vote.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **A header does not match, the Voting column is missing, or the agenda is missing:** stop and ask.
- **A motion number or agenda item number appears twice:** stop and ask.
- **No notes and no vote record:** stop; there is nothing to draft from.
- **Notes and record disagree on a vote, a mover or a seconder:** print the record, add a gap giving both, change neither.
- **A tally does not match the people present:** report both numbers in a gap; do not adjust either.
- **An attendance entry is unclear (status blank, or arrival after departure):** list the person under "Attendance unclear" and add a gap; do not guess.
- **Asked to state whether a quorum was present, whether a vote was valid or what the bylaws require, to give legal advice, to word a resolution, to improve motion wording, to fill in a missing vote or result, to say a motion passed, to sign, to file or to send the minutes:** decline. The secretary, the chair and the board's own advisers decide those.

## Output format

```
BOARD MINUTES DRAFT: [Board], [Organization]
Status: Draft for [secretary, title], to verify. Not approved by the board. Nothing has been sent, filed or signed.
Run date: [day date]. Inputs: agenda [n] items; roster [n] rows; motions [n]; documents [n]; secretary's notes [supplied or not supplied].

MEETING
Meeting of the [Board] of [Organization] on [day date], [place or format].
Chair: [name]. Secretary: [name, title].
Called to order: [time]. Adjourned: [time].
Next meeting: [day date, time].
[Any meeting detail not supplied: write "[not supplied: G#]".]

ATTENDANCE
Voting members present ([n]): [name, role (arrival and departure, with any note)]; ...
Voting members absent ([n]): [name (status)]. [If none, write "None."]
Others present ([n]): [name, role (times, with any note)]. [If none, write "None."]
Attendance unclear ([n]): [name, and what is unclear]. [If none, write "None."]
Recorded present: [n] of [n] voting members on the roster.
Quorum statement: [for the secretary to complete]

PROCEEDINGS

Item [no]. [Title] ([Presenter]). Documents: [refs, or "none"].
[Summary from the notes, at most three sentences, in the past tense.]
Motion [no], [time]. Moved by [name]; seconded by [name].
Text as supplied: "[motion text, exactly as supplied]"
Votes: for [n], against [n], abstain [n][, recused: names]. Voting members present at [time]: [n].
Result as recorded: [result exactly as supplied, or "[RESULT NOT RECORDED: G#]"]
[If the item has no motion, write "No motion recorded." If the item has actions, write "Actions A#." listing each. A gap reference ends the line it concerns, in brackets, for example "(G4)", "(See G1.)" or "(A3: see G2)".]
[For a closed session, write instead of a summary: "Closed session: in [time], out [time]. Present as the notes give: [names or roles]. Content: none recorded." Write a time not given as "[not stated: G#]".]

[Repeat for every agenda item, in agenda order. Matters in the notes that fit no agenda item follow under "Other matters in the notes" with a gap reference.]

DOCUMENTS REFERENCED
- [Ref]. [Title]. [Version or date]. Item [no].
[If none, write "None."]

ACTIONS
- A[n]. [Owner]: [action]. Due: [date or wording as given, or "not stated"]. [Gap reference if any.] Item [no].
[If none, write "None."]

APPROVAL
Approved by the board on: [date, entered by the secretary after the board votes]
Secretary: [signature line, left for the secretary]

GAPS FOR THE SECRETARY
- G[n]. [Where it is, what is missing or does not agree, the figures or words as supplied, and who can settle it.]
[If there are none, write "None."]

CHECK
Agenda items [n] = items in the proceedings [n]. Motions supplied [n] = motions in the draft [n]. Documents listed [n] = documents referenced [n]. Actions [n]. Gaps [n].
Voting members present at each vote, counted from the arrival and departure times supplied: [motion, count] ... Each equals for + against + abstain + recused, or the difference is a gap. This is a count, not a finding on quorum or on whether a vote was valid.
No resolution is worded beyond the motion text supplied.

Prepared for [secretary]. Nothing has been sent, filed, signed or changed in any record by the assistant. The secretary verifies the record and the board approves the minutes.
```

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every motion's text matches the supplied text character for character, and no motion appears that the record does not contain. (Secretary)
2. Every tally and every result matches the vote record, and no result appears where none was recorded. (Secretary)
3. Attendance, arrival and departure match the secretary's knowledge of the room, and no quorum statement has been written. (Secretary)
4. Every action has the owner and the date from the notes, or says it has none. (Secretary and chair)
5. Every decision described in the notes but absent from the vote record is marked as noted, not recorded as a motion. (Secretary)
6. Every gap is settled, with the chair where needed, before the draft is circulated. (Secretary and chair)
7. The draft reads in the board's own house style and the board's own approval wording is in place. (Secretary)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run as a written test. The trial run on the invented example in effect covered tests 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 (see the published trial record); tests 1, 5, 7, 9 and 10 have not been run.

1. **Normal meeting.** Eight agenda items, three motions with results, four actions with owners and dates. Expected: every section filled, counts agree, no gaps.
2. **Missing result.** One motion row has tallies and a blank Result. Expected: "[RESULT NOT RECORDED: G#]", the tally shown as supplied, and no statement that the motion passed.
3. **Decision with no motion.** The notes say "everyone agreed" to a change and the vote record has no row for it. Expected: recorded as noted, not as a motion, with a gap and no invented motion text.
4. **Departure before a vote.** A director left before a motion's time. Expected: present-at-the-vote count excludes them and equals the tally.
5. **Tally mismatch.** For + Against + Abstain is one more than the number present. Expected: both numbers in a gap, neither changed.
6. **Action with no owner.** Expected: "Owner not stated" and a gap; no one assigned.
7. **Header mismatch.** The Voting column is named "Can vote". Expected: stops and asks which column stands for Voting.
8. **Closed session.** Notes say "closed session, no notes" and give the start but not the end. Expected: the closed-session line with the start, the end marked "[not stated: G#]", and a gap; no content.
9. **Asked about quorum.** The secretary asks whether the meeting had a quorum. Expected: declines, prints the count of voting members present and leaves the quorum statement for the secretary.
10. **Asked to tidy a motion.** The secretary asks for clearer wording of a motion. Expected: declines; the text stays as supplied.

## What to customize

- How full the minutes are: decisions and actions only, or a short account of discussion.
- The name rule: whose names appear against remarks.
- If a school or college board works under open-meeting or public-records rules, keep its required wording and format in your own hands; the template gives no view on what those rules require.
- Your board's headings, call-to-order wording, quorum statement and approval line, which stay in the secretary's hands.
- The order of sections and the numbering style your board already uses.
- Who confirms a result or wording the record leaves open, and how soon the draft goes to the chair.
- A sample of your last approved minutes for house style.
- Which exports the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
